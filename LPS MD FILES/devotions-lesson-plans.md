#### Monday: Meeting Zechariah — an impossible promise

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 1:5–22  
**Topic:** Meeting Zechariah — an impossible promise

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that God keeps his promises, even ones that seem impossible.
- We are learning who Zechariah and Elizabeth were, and the promise the angel Gabriel brought them.
- I can explain why believing an impossible promise can be hard — even with proof right in front of you.

**Assessment:** Listen for participation in the opening belief question. Note which students can recall Zechariah's name, his situation, and connect it back to the 'would you believe a superpower claim' opener by the end of the session.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Welcome & hook** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Welcome the class back warmly — this is the first day of a brand-new term and a brand-new Bible story. “Welcome back, everyone! This term we’re going to meet someone amazing and important. He’s a Super Saviour!” Build a little excitement and mystery — don’t reveal who yet.

2. **Opening question — would you believe it?** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: This question is the setup for the whole story, so don't rush it. Push gently on "what would it take to convince you" — most students will land on "I'd need to see it for myself." Hold onto that thought out loud: "Keep that in mind, because in today's story, someone gets exactly that kind of proof — and still has a hard time believing it."
   - Key question: If someone told you they had a real superpower, would you believe them? What would it take to convince you?
   - Teacher guidance: Take a few quick responses. Push gently: most kids will say they'd need to SEE proof. Hold onto this — it's exactly the position Zechariah is about to be in, except he gets proof from an actual angel and still struggles to believe it.

3. **Introducing the comic & Zechariah** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: “This term we’ll be reading our very own Bible comic. It begins with a man named Zechariah.” Show the first comic panel. Explain who Zechariah and Elizabeth were — an older married couple who were never able to have children, which in their culture and time carried real sadness.
   - Activity note: Show comic panel 01-zechariah-elizabeth-intro on screen as you introduce them.

4. **Gabriel’s promise & the silence** _(4–5 min)_
   - Teacher talk: “One day, as Zechariah was serving God in the temple, and all the people were praying outside, the angel Gabriel appeared to him!” Show panel 02 — the angel’s promise. Then show panel 03 — the crowd’s confusion as Zechariah comes out unable to speak. Callback to the opening question: Zechariah got the most convincing proof possible — an actual angel, face to face — and still doubted. Build suspense; don’t explain yet exactly what he said or did wrong (revealed Day 2 — he doubted the angel).
   - Activity note: Show comic panels 02-gabriel-in-temple and 03-crowd-cant-speak in sequence.
   - Key question: Zechariah saw an actual angel and heard the promise himself — so why do you think he still struggled to believe it, and what do you think happened to him inside the temple that made him unable to speak?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept any responses — we're building anticipation, not confirming the answer yet. The full reveal comes tomorrow (he doubted the angel's promise about John, even after seeing him face to face).

**Where to next:** Day 2 reveals why Zechariah lost his voice, and introduces Mary and the angel’s announcement about Jesus.


#### Tuesday: Two surprising babies

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 1:24–38  
**Topic:** Two surprising babies

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning what happened to Zechariah's voice, and why.
- We are learning that God announced both John and Jesus before they were even born.
- I can name John and Jesus and begin to wonder who they are and why their births mattered.

**Assessment:** Check that students can recall why John's birth was extraordinary and can name both babies by the end of the session.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Recap & reveal** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: “Last time, Zechariah had just seen an angel — and now he couldn’t speak! The angel had told him that he and his wife Elizabeth, who were very old and had never been able to have children, would have a son. They were to name him John.” Show panel 04. Note for yourself (not necessarily stated outright unless asked): Zechariah lost his voice because he doubted the angel’s promise — you can confirm this if students ask directly.
   - Activity note: Show comic panel 04-elizabeth-pregnant.

2. **Why John's birth mattered** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Pause on the wonder of this moment for Elizabeth before moving on to Mary's story.
   - Key question: Why was John's birth so extraordinary?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: his parents were very old and had never been able to have children before — this was humanly impossible.

3. **Gabriel visits Mary** _(4–5 min)_
   - Teacher talk: “About one month later, God sent his angel Gabriel to visit a young woman named Mary.” Show panels 05–07 in sequence, reading each line aloud with feeling — these are the angel’s actual words to Mary.
   - Activity note: Show comic panels 05-gabriel-visits-mary, 06-dont-be-afraid-jesus, and 07-jesus-will-be-great in sequence.

4. **Naming the babies** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Check the class has caught both names before moving into the open wonder question.
   - Key question: What were the names of these two important babies?
   - Teacher guidance: John and Jesus.

5. **Open wonder question** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Close today’s session by opening the big question without answering it — this is meant to stay unresolved until later in the week.
   - Key question: Who do you think John is? Who do you think Jesus is?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept any guesses — this is genuinely open at this point in the story; we’ll build the answer across the week.

**Where to next:** Day 3 reveals more of what the angel said about Jesus becoming King, and shows his birth.


#### Wednesday: A promised King is born

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 1:30–35; 2:1–7  
**Topic:** A promised King is born

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that the angel announced Jesus would be an eternal King.
- We are learning that Mary trusted God even though she didn't understand how it would happen.
- I can say this week's memory verse and explain what it means in my own words.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can repeat the memory verse with reasonable accuracy by the end of the activity, and whether they can articulate what made John's and Jesus's births unusual.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)
- Memory verse displayed on screen (built into the app's verse slide)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Recap & the King promise** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: “An angel told Mary she would have a very special son, named Jesus. Let’s hear what else the angel said about him.” Show panel 08, reading the angel’s words aloud with weight — this is the heart of the promise: an eternal kingdom.
   - Activity note: Show comic panel 08-kingdom-never-end.

2. **Mary's question & the angel's answer** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show panel 09. Point out that Mary asked a very reasonable question (“How will this happen?”) and that the angel's answer pointed to God's power, not a normal explanation.
   - Activity note: Show comic panel 09-how-will-this-happen.

3. **Jesus is born** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: “Mary believed the angel and trusted God. Some time later, baby Jesus was born.” Show panels 10 and 11. Land on the line “Nothing is impossible for God!” as the takeaway image/moment.
   - Activity note: Show comic panels 10-nativity and 11-baby-jesus.

4. **Memory verse** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Introduce the verse slide (Luke 1:32–33, CEV). Read it together as a class two or three times, then invite the class to try saying it once without looking at the screen.
   - Activity note: Use the built-in verse block, then run the say-it-together activity that follows it in the app.

5. **Closing question** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Close by drawing the comparison between an ordinary birth and what made John's and Jesus's so different.
   - Key question: What's normally needed for a baby to be born — and what was different about John and Jesus?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: babies normally just come from a mum and dad; these two were announced in advance, by an angel, and given specific important jobs before they were even born.

**Where to next:** Day 4 brings it all together — who John and Jesus are, and what each was born to do.

**Memory Verse:**
> “He will be great and will be called the Son of God Most High. The Lord God will make him king, as his ancestor David was. He will rule the people of Israel forever, and his kingdom will never end.” — *Luke 1:32–33 (CEV)*

#### Monday: Getting ready for someone important

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 3:1–6  
**Topic:** Getting ready for someone important

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that John's job was to get people ready for Jesus' arrival.
- We are learning this week's memory verse and what it means to 'turn back to God.'
- I can explain why John's message of repentance and baptism mattered.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain, in their own words, what job John had — note who can connect 'getting ready' to John preparing people for Jesus by the end of the session.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Dinner-guest hook** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Open with the imagined scenario — really let students picture it. "Imagine someone really important was suddenly coming to your house for dinner tonight." Push for specifics: would they clean their room? Change clothes? The point to land softly (don't over-explain yet) is that getting ready properly takes real action, not just good intentions.
   - Key question: Imagine someone really important was suddenly coming to your house for dinner tonight. What would you do to get ready?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept any responses — cleaning up, tidying their room, putting on best clothes, etc. The point is that getting ready takes real effort and action, not just words.

2. **Reintroducing John** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: "Last term we met John — the baby whose birth was announced by an angel before he was even born. Now John is grown up, and he has a very important job: getting people ready for someone far more important than a dinner guest." This is a deliberate callback to Week 1 — give students a moment to remember John if they need it.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-01-john-in-desert.

3. **John's message** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the next panel and read John's words aloud with some weight — this is the heart of his preaching. Introduce this week's memory verse here directly from the panel's words.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-02-john-preaching-river, then introduce the verse slide (Luke 3:3, CEV).

4. **Isaiah's prophecy** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the Isaiah panel. Point out that this prophecy was written 700 years before John was even born — God had a plan for John's job long, long in advance.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-03-isaiah-prophecy.

5. **Closing question** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Close by drawing the link explicit between the opening hook and John's message — getting ready for God isn't just saying sorry, it's a real change. Don't fully resolve this; it's the bridge into tomorrow's lesson on 'producing fruit.'
   - Key question: John's message wasn't just words — people had to actually do something. Why do you think 'getting ready' for God needed more than just saying sorry?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: real change shows up in what we do, not just what we say. This sets up tomorrow's 'fruit' teaching directly.

**Where to next:** Day 2 unpacks what 'getting ready' actually looks like in action — through John's teaching about fruit trees and his specific instructions to the crowds.

**Memory Verse:**
> “Turn back to God and be baptised! Then your sins will be forgiven.” — *Luke 3:3 (CEV)*


#### Tuesday: What 'getting ready' looks like

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 3:7–18  
**Topic:** What 'getting ready' looks like

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that John used the picture of fruit trees to teach what real repentance looks like.
- We are learning that John gave specific, practical instructions to different groups of people.
- I can name one specific action that would show real change in my own life.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain the fruit-tree metaphor in their own words, and whether they can name a concrete (not vague) example of 'producing fruit' during the activity.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Recap & fruit trees** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: "Yesterday, John told everyone to turn back to God. But what does that actually look like? John had an answer." Show the fruit-tree panel and let the metaphor land before asking the question.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-04-fruit-trees-teaching.

2. **Unpacking the metaphor** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: This question does the real teaching work — don't rush past it. Make sure the class actually arrives at 'you'd judge by what it produces' before moving on.
   - Key question: If a tree never grew any fruit, how would you know just by looking at it whether it was healthy or not?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: you'd know by what it actually produces, not by what it looks like on the outside. That's John's point — real change in us shows up in what we actually do.

3. **John's specific instructions** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show both panels in sequence. Point out that John didn't give the same instruction to everyone — he gave specific, practical advice depending on each group's actual situation and temptations.
   - Activity note: Show comic images wk2-05-what-should-we-do and wk2-06-tax-collectors-soldiers in sequence.

4. **Personal application activity** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: This is the activity where the lesson becomes personal. Push for something concrete and specific rather than a vague good intention — "be nicer" doesn't count, "share my lunch with someone who doesn't have one" does.
   - Activity note: John gave different people different instructions, but the idea was the same each time. Invite a few students to share their specific 'fruit' idea for the week.

5. **Someone more powerful is coming** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the final panel of the day. Let the question hang — this is meant to make students do the connecting work themselves rather than telling them outright.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-07-could-john-be-king.
   - Key question: John just said someone more powerful than him is coming. After everything we know from last term, who do you think that is?
   - Teacher guidance: Jesus — this is a deliberate callback. Let students make the connection themselves before confirming.

**Where to next:** Day 3 shows Jesus himself arriving at the river to be baptised by John.


#### Wednesday: Jesus is baptised

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 3:21–22  
**Topic:** Jesus is baptised

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that Jesus came to the same river where John was baptising people.
- We are learning what happened immediately after Jesus was baptised — the Holy Spirit and the voice from heaven.
- I can say this week's memory verse and explain what it means in my own words.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can describe what happened at Jesus' baptism (the dove, the voice) and whether they can repeat the memory verse with reasonable accuracy.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)
- Memory verse displayed on screen (built into the app's verse slide)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Jesus arrives** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: "John told everyone someone more powerful than him was coming. One day, that someone arrived — at the very same river." Show the arrival panel and let the moment build a little before revealing what happens.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-08-jesus-arrives-at-river.

2. **Jesus is baptised** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the baptism panel, then ask the puzzle question. This is meant to provoke real thinking — Jesus didn't need forgiveness the way everyone else did, so why be baptised? Don't over-resolve it; a short class discussion is enough.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-09-jesus-baptised.
   - Key question: John had been telling everyone to turn back to God and be baptised because of their sins. So why do you think Jesus — who hadn't done anything wrong — wanted to be baptised too?
   - Teacher guidance: This is genuinely open and a bit puzzling on purpose. Accept any thoughtful guesses; the main point for today is what happens next, not fully resolving this question.

3. **The Holy Spirit and the voice** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the dove/voice panel and read the heavenly voice's words aloud with weight — this is one of the most important moments in the whole Gospel of Luke, confirming exactly who Jesus is.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk2-10-holy-spirit-descends.

4. **Memory verse** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Bring back this week's verse slide (Luke 3:3). Read it together two or three times, then invite the class to try it once without looking.
   - Activity note: Use the built-in verse block, then run the say-it-together activity that follows it in the app.

5. **Closing check** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Quick recall question to close — make sure the key detail (whose voice, what it said) has landed before moving to tomorrow's wrap-up.
   - Key question: Whose voice spoke from heaven, and what did the voice call Jesus?
   - Teacher guidance: God's voice — calling Jesus his Son, whom he loves.

**Where to next:** Day 4 brings the week together — what John's job was, and what Jesus' baptism revealed about who he is.

**Memory Verse:**
> “Turn back to God and be baptised! Then your sins will be forgiven.” — *Luke 3:3 (CEV)*

#### Monday: Jesus says who he is

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 4:14–21  
**Topic:** Jesus says who he is

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that Jesus went back to his hometown synagogue and read a promise from Isaiah about a coming rescuer.
- We are learning that Jesus directly claimed this promise was about himself.
- I can explain what kind of people Jesus said he came for.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain, in their own words, that Jesus stood up and claimed the Isaiah promise was about himself — note who can name at least two groups of people the passage says he came for.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)
- Memory verse displayed on screen (built into the app's verse slide)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **The long wait** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Open with the personal question — let a few students share something they waited a long time for. Don't rush this; the feeling of anticipation finally paying off is the emotional anchor for the whole week.
   - Key question: Has there ever been something you waited for ages to happen — and then it actually did? What was that like?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept any answer — a birthday, a holiday, a long-awaited event. The point is the feeling of a long wait finally being over, which is exactly the feeling we're building toward today.

2. **Recap and set-up** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Briefly recap the story so far across the term: John got everyone ready, Jesus was baptised, God called him his Son. Today we find out what Jesus himself has to say about who he is.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk3-01-jesus-walks-to-nazareth.

3. **Jesus reads the scroll** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the synagogue panel, then bring up the verse slide and read it together slowly — this is a dense, important passage. Take time over it rather than rushing through.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk3-02-reads-isaiah-scroll, then the verse slide (Luke 4:18–19, CEV).
   - Key question: Jesus reads a list of things this promised person was supposed to do. What kinds of people does the list say he's come for?
   - Teacher guidance: The poor, prisoners, the blind, everyone who suffers — people who are struggling or stuck. Jesus is describing someone who rescues and restores.

4. **Today, in front of you** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Show the final panel and read Jesus' line aloud with weight: "What you have heard me read has come true today." Make sure students grasp the size of the claim — Jesus isn't just teaching about an old prophecy, he's saying it's about him, right now, in that room.
   - Activity note: Show comic image wk3-03-fulfilled-today.
   - Key question: Jesus didn't just read an old promise — he said it about himself, right there, out loud. What is Jesus actually claiming about who he is?
   - Teacher guidance: Jesus is claiming to be the promised rescuer-King the Scriptures were talking about — God's chosen one, sent to save and restore people. This is a huge, direct claim.

**Where to next:** Day 2 shows how the crowd in his own hometown actually responds to this huge claim — and it isn't what you'd expect.

**Memory Verse:**
> “The Lord’s Spirit has come to me, because he has chosen me to tell the good news to the poor. The Lord has sent me to announce freedom for prisoners, to give sight to the blind, to free everyone who suffers, and to say, ‘This is the year the Lord has chosen.’” — *Luke 4:18–19 (CEV)*


#### Tuesday: The hometown crowd turns

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Luke 4:22–24  
**Topic:** The hometown crowd turns

**Big Idea:** Jesus is God's powerful Son who fulfils God's promises.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning how the people of Nazareth responded to Jesus' claim about himself.
- We are learning that familiarity with Jesus led to doubt rather than belief for many in his hometown.
- I can explain why Jesus didn't perform miracles in Nazareth.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain that the crowd's familiarity with Jesus — knowing him as 'just' Joseph's son — made them doubt rather than believe his claim.

**Resources:**
- Screen/device to display the comic panels (already embedded in the app)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Recap yesterday's claim** _(1–2 min)_

#### Monday: God's promise to his people

**Passage:** Exodus 19:1–8  
**Topic:** God's promise to his people

**Big Idea:** God rescued Israel out of slavery in Egypt, and now, at Mount Sinai, he gives them his Law to show his people how to live as his special, treasured people — not to earn his rescue, but because he had already saved them.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that God led Israel to Mount Sinai and offered them a special covenant relationship.
- We are learning that Israel agreed to obey everything the Lord had said.
- I can explain what it means that Israel would be God's 'treasured possession'.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain, in their own words, what God offered Israel at Sinai and how the people responded.

**Teaching Script:**

1. **What comes after a rescue?** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Recap last week's rescue at the Red Sea, then ask the warm-up question.
   - Key question: Last week we saw God rescue Israel out of Egypt and through the Red Sea. If God has just done something amazing to save you, what do you think he might want from you next?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses. Guide toward: not payment or earning the rescue back, but a relationship — as we'll see today, God's answer was to invite Israel into a special relationship with him.

2. **Arriving at Sinai** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Tell how Israel travelled to Mount Sinai and Moses went up to meet with God.

3. **God's treasured possession** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Read God's covenant offer to the people, then ask what being God's 'treasured possession' would mean.
   - Key question: What do you think it would mean to be God's 'treasured possession' out of every nation on earth?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses. Guide toward: being specially chosen, loved, and set apart by God — not because Israel had earned it, but because God graciously chose them.

4. **'We will do everything the Lord has said'** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Tell how the people responded together, then introduce the memory verse (carried over from last week).
   - Activity note: Use the built-in verse block for Deuteronomy 4:39, then the say-it-together activity block.

**Where to next:** Day 2 finds out exactly what God's Law said, starting with the Ten Commandments.

**Memory Verse:**
> “So remember that the Lord is the only true God, whether in the sky above or on the earth below.” — *Deuteronomy 4:39 (CEV)*


#### Tuesday: Loving God first

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Exodus 20:1–8  
**Topic:** Loving God first

**Big Idea:** God rescued Israel out of slavery in Egypt, and now, at Mount Sinai, he gives them his Law to show his people how to live as his special, treasured people — not to earn his rescue, but because he had already saved them.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning the first four of the Ten Commandments, which are about our relationship with God.
- We are learning that these commandments teach Israel to worship only the Lord, and to keep the Sabbath as belonging to him.
- I can explain why commandments about God come before commandments about people.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can name at least one of the first four commandments and explain, broadly, what it teaches about our relationship with God.

**Teaching Script:**

1. **What comes first?** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask the warm-up question before revealing how the Ten Commandments actually begin.
   - Key question: If you were making a list of rules for how to live well, what do you think should come first on the list?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses. Use this to set up how the Ten Commandments begin — not with rules about people, but about our relationship with God first.

2. **The first four commandments** _(4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Read through the first four commandments together, about worship, idols, God's name, and the Sabbath.

3. **Why God first?** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask why commandments about God come before commandments about people.
   - Key question: Why do you think God put commandments about himself first, before commandments about how to treat other people?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: our relationship with God comes first and shapes everything else — how we treat others flows out of how we treat God.

4. **Memory verse** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Say the memory verse together again.
   - Activity note: Use the say-it-together activity block.

**Where to next:** Day 3 covers the rest of the Ten Commandments — how to treat other people.

**Memory Verse:**
> “So remember that the Lord is the only true God, whether in the sky above or on the earth below.” — *Deuteronomy 4:39 (CEV)*


#### Wednesday: Loving our neighbour

**Passage:** Exodus 20:12–17; 19:3–8 (recap)  
**Topic:** Loving our neighbour

**Big Idea:** God rescued Israel out of slavery in Egypt, and now, at Mount Sinai, he gives them his Law to show his people how to live as his special, treasured people — not to earn his rescue, but because he had already saved them.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning the last six of the Ten Commandments, which are about how to treat other people.
- We are learning that Israel agreed to keep God's Law only after God had already rescued them.
- I can explain why the rescue came before the Law, not after it.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain that obedience was Israel's grateful response to being rescued, not a way of earning God's rescue.

**Teaching Script:**

1. **The last six commandments** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Read through the last six commandments together, about how to treat other people.

2. **The hardest one** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask which commandment students think would be hardest to keep, and why.
   - Key question: Which of these six commandments do you think would have been the hardest for people to keep, and why?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept any reasonable answer with a real reason. There's no wrong answer here — the goal is for students to notice these commandments are still genuinely hard to keep today.

3. **Rescue before Law** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Recap that Israel agreed to the Law only after being rescued, then ask why the order matters.
   - Key question: Why do you think it mattered that God rescued Israel before he gave them the Law, rather than the other way around?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: obedience wasn't how Israel earned their rescue — the rescue came first. Keeping the Law was meant to be a grateful response to what God had already done for them, not a way of earning it.

4. **Memory verse** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Try the memory verse together without looking, if the class is ready.

   - Key question: What has God shown us about how to live today?

#### Monday: God wants to live with his people

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Exodus 25:1–9  
**Topic:** God wants to live with his people

**Big Idea:** God chose to live among his people in the tabernacle — but because God is holy and his people are sinful, he also gave detailed instructions for priests and sacrifices, a way to be made right with him. This whole system points forward to Jesus, whose death lets us come into God's presence forever.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that God wanted to live among his people, and asked them to build him a sacred tent.
- We are learning that God gave Moses an exact pattern to follow for the tabernacle.
- I can begin learning this week's new memory verse, Psalm 117:2.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain why God wanted a tabernacle, and why building it exactly to the pattern mattered.

**Teaching Script:**

1. **A home for God?** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask the warm-up question before describing the tabernacle.
   - Key question: If God wanted to actually live among his people, right in the middle of their camp, what do you think that tent or building would need to be like?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses. Use this to set up just how detailed and careful God's instructions for the tabernacle were.

2. **'Make me a sacred tent'** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Tell how God asked for gifts to build the tabernacle, so he could live among his people.

3. **An exact pattern** _(3–4 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Describe how detailed God's instructions were, then ask why the exact pattern mattered.
   - Key question: Why do you think it mattered so much that Moses build the tabernacle exactly to God's pattern, instead of using his own ideas?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward Hebrews 8:1–5: the tabernacle was a copy of something real in heaven, so getting the earthly copy exactly right mattered — it wasn't just decoration, it showed something true about God.

4. **New memory verse** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Introduce this week's new memory verse, Psalm 117:2, and try saying it together.
   - Activity note: Use the built-in verse block for Psalm 117:2, then the say-it-together activity block.

**Where to next:** Day 2 looks at why priests and sacrifices were needed before anyone could enter God's presence.

**Memory Verse:**
> “His love for us is wonderful, his faithfulness never ends. Shout praises to the Lord!” — *Psalm 117:2 (CEV)*


#### Tuesday: Priests and sacrifices

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Exodus 40 (overview)  
**Topic:** Priests and sacrifices

**Big Idea:** God chose to live among his people in the tabernacle — but because God is holy and his people are sinful, he also gave detailed instructions for priests and sacrifices, a way to be made right with him. This whole system points forward to Jesus, whose death lets us come into God's presence forever.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that the tabernacle had separate areas, with the most holy place reserved for God's presence.
- We are learning that priests and sacrifices were needed because of the seriousness of sin.
- I can explain why not just anyone could enter the most holy place.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain, simply, why priests and sacrifices were necessary before anyone could approach God.

**Teaching Script:**

1. **Areas of the tabernacle** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Describe the courtyard, holy place, and most holy place, and who could go where.

2. **Priests and sacrifices** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Explain the role of priests and daily sacrifices in dealing with the people's sin.
   - Key question: Why do you think God set up such careful rules about who could come near him, and how?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: God's holiness and people's sinfulness don't mix — the sacrifices and priesthood were God's own gracious way of dealing with sin so his people could still be near him safely, not humans just making it hard for themselves.

3. **How seriously God takes sin** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask what the sacrifice system tells us about how seriously God takes sin.
   - Key question: What does this tell us about how seriously God takes sin?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses. Guide toward: sin is a genuinely serious problem that needed real dealing with — through the blood of an animal sacrifice, standing in for the person's sin, every single day.

4. **Memory verse** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Say this week's memory verse together again.
   - Activity note: Use the say-it-together activity block.

**Where to next:** Day 3 finds out how Jesus' death changed all of this, once and for all.

**Memory Verse:**
> “His love for us is wonderful, his faithfulness never ends. Shout praises to the Lord!” — *Psalm 117:2 (CEV)*


#### Wednesday: Jesus, our once-for-all sacrifice

**Duration:** 10–15 minutes  
**Passage:** Mark 15:37–39; Hebrews 10:11–14  
**Topic:** Jesus, our once-for-all sacrifice

**Big Idea:** God chose to live among his people in the tabernacle — but because God is holy and his people are sinful, he also gave detailed instructions for priests and sacrifices, a way to be made right with him. This whole system points forward to Jesus, whose death lets us come into God's presence forever.

**Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that the temple curtain tore in two when Jesus died, from top to bottom.
- We are learning that Jesus offered himself once for all as our great high priest, so the old sacrifice system was no longer needed.
- I can explain how Jesus' death is different from the priests' sacrifices.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can connect the torn curtain to what Jesus' death achieved, and explain why his sacrifice was 'once for all'.

**Teaching Script:**

1. **The curtain tears** _(2–3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Tell how the temple curtain tore when Jesus died, then ask why the direction of the tear matters.
   - Key question: Why do you think it's significant that the curtain tore from top to bottom, rather than being ripped from the bottom by a person?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: it showed this was God's own doing, not a human tearing it — signalling that the barrier between God and his people had been dealt with by God himself, through Jesus.

2. **Our great high priest** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Explain that Jesus offered himself once for all as the perfect sacrifice.

3. **Once for all** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask how Jesus' sacrifice is different from what the priests did about sin.
   - Key question: How is what Jesus did different from what the priests did about sin?
   - Teacher guidance: Guide toward: priests could never take away sin, only cover it temporarily — but Jesus' one sacrifice deals with sin completely and forever, so that people who trust him are set free from sin.

4. **Memory verse** _(1–2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Say this week's memory verse together once more.
   - Activity note: Use the say-it-together activity block.

**Where to next:** Day 4 looks forward to when God's people will finally live with him face to face, forever.

**Memory Verse:**
> “His love for us is wonderful, his faithfulness never ends. Shout praises to the Lord!” — *Psalm 117:2 (CEV)*

**Passage:** Revelation 21:1–4

**Big Idea:** God chose to live among his people in the tabernacle — but because God is holy and his people are sinful, he also gave detailed instructions for priests and sacrifices, a way to be made right with him. **Learning Intentions:**
- We are learning that Revelation promises a future where God will live with his people forever, face to face.
- We are learning that this future is what the whole tabernacle story, and Jesus' sacrifice, were pointing toward all along.
- I can say this week's memory verse, Psalm 117:2, fully from memory.

**Assessment:** Listen for whether students can explain, in their own words, how the tabernacle story ends with the promise in Revelation 21.

**Resources:**
- CEV Bible (optional, for reading Revelation 21:1–4 aloud)

**Teaching Script:**

1. **The whole week, and beyond** _(2 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Recap the week's journey from the tabernacle to Jesus.

2. **God's home with his people** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Read the promise from Revelation 21, then ask students to imagine what it will be like.
   - Key question: What do you think it will be like to live with God face to face, with no more sin, sadness, or separation between us and him?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses — encourage genuine imagination here. Guide toward: this is what the whole tabernacle system was pointing forward to all along — perfect, permanent closeness with God, made possible through Jesus.

3. **Real hope** _(3 min)_
   - Teacher talk: Ask how this future promise changes the way we think about following Jesus today.
   - Key question: How does knowing this future change the way we think about following Jesus today?
   - Teacher guidance: Accept responses. Guide toward: it gives us real hope — everything broken about our relationship with God because of sin will one day be completely fixed.

4. **Memory verse and closing prayer** _(2 min)_
