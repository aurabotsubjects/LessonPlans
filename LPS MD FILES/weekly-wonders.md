## Week 1

**Question:** This remains the same size throughout our lifetime.

**Answer:** Your Eyes

**Facts:**
1. The human eye blinks an average of 4,200,000 times a year. This means if you were given 10 cents for every time you blinked you would make $420,000 annually.
2. Your eye is the fastest muscle in your body. Hence, the phrase: “In the blink of an eye.”
3. Your iris has 256 unique characteristics, while your fingerprint only has 40.

**Facts:**

## Week 6

**Question:** You are more likely to see this in the Summer than the Winter.

**Answer:** Rainbows

**Facts:**
1. You are less likely to see a rainbow in the winter – but why? Because of snow! A rainbow is the result of light passing through a spectrum – in most cases, a collection of raindrops.
2. The average rainbow is observable for less than an hour. But in 2017 in Taiwan, people observed a rainbow for 8 hours and 58 minutes.
3. A double rainbow appears when light is reflected twice in a raindrop. The secondary rainbow – which will be a little bit higher and fainter in color – will actually have its colors reversed.

## Week 7

**Question:** This invertebrate was once displayed in a museum thought to be dead until it started moving several years later.

**Answer:** Snails

**Facts:**
1. Certain land snails can sleep for up to three years in hibernation, but generally, snails will sleep on and off in between periods of 13 to 15 hours. Afterwards, they experience a sudden jolt of energy for the next 30 hours, where they get all their snail chores done!
2. The speed of snails is around 12-20 mm per second. If they moved without stopping, it would take more than a week to complete 1 kilometer.
3. The mucus of the garden snail is used to treat wrinkles, spots, and scars on the skin.

## Week 63

## Week 65

**Question:** In the right environment, these are known to live 100 years on average and can grow up to 46 metres high.

**Answer:** Pine Trees (Christmas Trees)

## Week 66

**Answer:** Perfume

## Week 67

**Question:** During World War II, a type of this plant fiber was used to stuff life jackets — about six times more buoyant than cork.

**Answer:** Weeds

## Week 68

**Question:** They have a highly specialized cardiovascular system to manage blood flow to their brains. Their hearts are incredibly strong and large — about 0.6 metres long and weighing around 11 kgs.

**Answer:** Giraffes

## Week 69

**Question:** A study by the University of Pennsylvania found that neighborhoods with more of these had lower crime rates.

**Answer:** Christmas Lights

**Facts:**

3. In 2014, a Christmas tree in Norway was decorated with 500,000 lights. The tree was over 200 feet tall and could be seen for miles.

## Week 70

**Question:** This sea creature has three hearts and blue blood.

## Week 71

**Question:** This snack can pop up to 3 feet into the air.

**Answer:** Popcorn

**Facts:**

## Week 72

**Question:** You actually can't fully sink in this, no matter what the movies show.

**Answer:** Quicksand

**Facts:**
1. Quicksand is just ordinary sand that's been saturated with water, which makes it behave like a liquid until you move.
2. Since your body is less dense than quicksand, you naturally float instead of sinking all the way — panicking and thrashing is what actually pulls people under.
3. The safest way out is to move slowly, lean back to spread your weight, and paddle toward firmer ground.

## Week 73

**Question:** Some of these shoot out strands of glass instead of lava.

**Answer:** Volcanoes

**Facts:**
1. Certain eruptions produce thin strands of volcanic glass nicknamed ‘Pele’s hair’, after the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes.
2. Volcanoes exist on other planets too — Mars is home to Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the entire solar system.
3. There are roughly 1,500 potentially active volcanoes around the world, not even counting the ones hidden underwater.

## Week 74

**Question:** Time moves slower the closer you get to one of these.

3. There's a supermassive black hole sitting at the very center of our own galaxy, the Milky Way.

## Week 75

**Question:** The very first versions of this ride were made of ice.

2. The first wheeled roller coaster was built in France after warmer weather made the ice slides impossible to keep running year-round.

## Week 77

**Question:** This piece of equipment was studied to help train pilots and astronauts.

## Week 78

**Question:** This sport started out being called ‘sidewalk surfing’.

## Week 79

**Question:** Despite the name, this creature isn't actually a worm.

**Answer:** Glow Worms

**Facts:**

## Week 80

**Question:** This plant can count to five.

**Answer:** Venus Flytraps

**Facts:**
1. A Venus flytrap's trap only snaps shut after something touches its trigger hairs twice within about 20 seconds — a built-in counter that stops it wasting energy on false alarms.
2. It takes several more touches after that before the trap actually starts digesting its prey.
3. Each individual trap can only close and reopen a handful of times before it stops working and falls off.

## Week 97

**Question:** This invisible force can make your hair stand straight up.

**Answer:** Static Electricity

**Facts:**

3. Dry winter air makes static shocks more common, since moisture in the air normally helps the charge drain away harmlessly.
